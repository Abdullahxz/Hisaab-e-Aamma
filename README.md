# Hisaab-e-Aamma: Pakistan Federal Budget 2026–27, Visualised

**Where does Pakistan's money come from, and where does it go?**

Every year, Pakistan's federal budget is published across thousands of pages of PDFs. The information is public, but it isn't accessible. Understanding something as basic as “how much do we spend on debt interest versus schools?” means digging through dense tables written for accountants, not citizens.

Hisaab-e-Aamma, which literally means public accounts takes the official federal budget documents and presents them in a form that is easier to explore, compare, and understand.

The budget is already public, this is one attempt to make budget information more accessible to the people whose money it describes.

## What you can explore

🌊 **The budget flow** - a single interactive diagram of the entire FY 2026-27 federal budget: taxes and borrowing flowing in, the constitutionally mandated NFC transfer to the provinces, and spending flowing out. Click any stream to drill deeper, from *FBR Tax Revenue* all the way down to excise duty on cement, or from *Current Expenditure* down to domestic versus foreign debt interest.

🏛️ **Ministry by ministry** - every federal ministry and division with its full allocation, the services it promises to deliver over a 5-year horizon, and, for the largest ministries, each parliamentary demand broken down by what the money actually buys: salaries, pensions, operating costs, subsidies, and assets.

🧾 **Your tax receipt** - enter the income tax you pay and get a personalised receipt showing how much goes to the provinces under the NFC Award and what the federal share funds, including debt interest, defence, pensions, and development.

📖 **Budget basics** - a plain-language primer and glossary. What is a "divisible pool"? Why is repaying debt not the same as spending? No economics degree required.

📊 **Context on every number** - year-over-year change against last year's budget and revised estimates, share of the total, and plain-language descriptions of what each line means.

🔗 **Shareable links** - every view you drill into has its own link. Send someone straight to "Subsidies → Power Sector" and they will see exactly what you saw.

## Why you can trust the numbers

Accuracy is the entire point. This project holds itself to three rules:

1. **Every figure links to its official source.** Each number cites the exact document, table, and page, with a direct link to the PDF on the Finance Division's own website (`finance.gov.pk`) opened at the cited page. You never have to take our word for anything.

2. **Everything must reconcile.** The budget flow balances to the rupee against the official *Budget at a Glance*. Breakdowns are only shown when they sum exactly to their published totals. Figures that could not be independently verified are omitted and marked, never guessed.

3. **Cross-checked across documents.** Headline figures are verified across multiple official publications, including the Budget in Brief, the Annual Budget Statement, and the Demands for Grants, before appearing here.

All data comes from the official Federal Budget 2026-27 documents published by the Finance Division, Government of Pakistan: <https://www.finance.gov.pk/fb_2026_27.html>

## Accuracy, independence & reuse

**This is not an official government product.** It is an independent, non-commercial visualisation built for public understanding. It carries no advertising and takes no position.

**Figures may contain errors.** Numbers are transcribed and computed from official PDFs by automated parsers with reconciliation checks. Transcription and interpretation mistakes are still possible. Every figure on the site links to the exact page of the document behind it, **verify against that source before relying on any number** for reporting, research, or any decision.
